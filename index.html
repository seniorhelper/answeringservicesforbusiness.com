Six questions before you sign with any answering service

1. What counts as a minute?

Ask whether hold time, after-call work and wrap-up are billed, and whether minutes round up to the next 30 or 60 seconds.

2. Can it book jobs?

A message taker sends you a note. A booking service puts the job on your calendar. Know which you're paying for.

3. How fast do leads reach you?

Text and email within a minute, with name, number, address and job type, not a daily summary.

4. What happens to web form leads?

Phone is half the story. If online leads wait hours for a reply, they go cold just like missed calls.

5. Are calls recorded, and is that disclosed?

Some states, including California, require every party's consent to record. The service should announce recording.

6. What's the exit?

Month-to-month, and your call recordings and lead data exported to you if you leave.

Speed matters more than polish

In a Harvard Business Review audit of 2,241 U.S. companies, only 37% responded to a web lead within an hour, the average reply took 42 hours, and firms that reached a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it. Whatever you pick, measure how many minutes pass between a call or form and a real conversation.

Calling leads back with AI? Answering inbound calls is fine. Calling or texting leads back with an AI or prerecorded voice generally needs prior express written consent under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, so your web forms need a clear, unchecked consent box that names your business. Answering service FAQ

How much does an answering service cost for a small business?

Live answering services commonly bill per minute or per call, and published small-business plans often land in the low hundreds of dollars a month and up depending on volume. AI receptionists are usually cheaper per call, from tens to a few hundred dollars a month. Compare using your own monthly call count and average call length.

Is an AI receptionist better than a live answering service?

AI handles volume, after-hours calls and simple booking cheaply and never puts callers on hold. Live agents handle upset callers, complicated jobs and judgment calls better. Many small businesses use AI for after-hours and overflow and keep a person for the rest.

How fast should I respond to a new lead?

Within the hour at most, and faster is better. A Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 U.S. companies found firms that contacted web leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited longer.

Is it legal to have an AI call leads back?

Answering calls people make to you is fine. Calling or texting leads back with an AI or prerecorded voice generally needs the person's prior express written consent under the TCPA, which is why your web forms need a clear consent checkbox.
